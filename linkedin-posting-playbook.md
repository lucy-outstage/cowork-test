# LinkedIn Posting Playbook — 2026 Best Practice

*A two-page field guide for marketing teams. Grounded in the 2026 algorithm shift.*

## The one thing to understand

LinkedIn now ranks content with **360Brew**, an LLM-based system (publicly confirmed in the LinkedIn Engineering feed article, March 2026). It moved distribution from a **social graph** (reach based on who you're connected to) to an **interest graph** (reach based on topical relevance and demonstrated expertise).

**What this means for us:**
- Raw reach is down platform-wide (~50% fewer views year-over-year), but *relevant* reach is up — posts now travel to strangers who care about your topic, not just your network.
- Every account gets a **topic fingerprint**. Post consistently in a niche and the engine routes you to people interested in that niche. Post scattershot and your content travels poorly.
- **Dwell time** (how long people linger on a post) is now the master signal. 0–3 seconds ≈ 1.2% engagement; 61+ seconds ≈ 15.6%. Everything below is really about buying attention-seconds.

## The five rules

### 1. Write for two readers: the human *and* the AI crawler
LinkedIn is now the **#2 most-cited source in AI search** — cited in ~14% of ChatGPT and ~13% of Google AI Mode responses to professional queries. B2B buyers ask AI tools early in their journey, and **crawlers only read plain text in the caption.** Text trapped inside an image, PDF, or carousel is invisible to them.
- ✅ Put your real substance in the caption, in plain language, using specific industry terms, frameworks, and metrics — this is what feeds both the topic fingerprint and AI citations.
- ❌ Don't rely on the visual to carry the insight. Don't use third-party "bold/italic" Unicode generators — they render as broken symbols crawlers can't parse and tank AI citation.

### 2. Length: aim for 1,300–2,500 characters
Across large post studies (300k+ posts), the **1,300–2,500 character** band earns the highest median engagement. Under ~400 characters, interest-graph routing largely ignores you.
- Treat 1,300–2,500 as the target; never publish "quick takes" under 400 as standalone posts.

### 3. Win the first line (the "See More" cutoff)
Mobile truncates at **~140 characters**, desktop at **~210**. Your hook — the counter-intuitive claim, the number, the tension — must land *before* the cutoff or the post never opens.
- First line = the whole value proposition in one breath. No throat-clearing.

### 4. Rotate three formats — roughly 60% text-with-image, 40% carousel
| Format | Why it works | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep-text + native image** | Small/mid accounts hit a reach ceiling on text alone; a visual gives "stopping power." Use *authentic* photos or un-templated charts — not ad-style graphics with heavy text overlays. | Default post. Insights, POV, stories. |
| **Document (PDF) carousel, 8–10 slides** | Highest human engagement of any format (~6.6%). Holds attention 30–60s and earns **Saves** (a top-weighted signal). Reach drops sharply past 10 slides. | Frameworks, how-tos, checklists, data. |
| **Text-only** | Works for large accounts with an active follower base; risky for growing accounts. | Established voices, quick reactions. |

### 5. Optimize for the high-value actions, not likes
The algorithm weights *depth* of interaction. In descending value:
1. **Substantive comments** (15+ words) — write posts that invite a real reply; then reply back to keep the thread alive.
2. **Saves** — number your frameworks and make posts worth bookmarking. Saves signal lasting reference value and drive outsized reach.
3. **Shares with commentary.**
4. Reactions/likes count least.

## Do / Don't (pin this above your publishing calendar)

**Do**
- Post 2–4× per week, consistently, in a tight topic lane.
- Front-load the hook; write 1,300–2,500 characters of plain-text substance.
- Design carousels 8–10 slides, numbered, one idea per slide.
- Ask a genuine question and reply to every substantive comment in the first hour.
- Skip hashtags — hashtag-free posts now perform 5–10% *better*; 360Brew reads your full copy instead.

**Don't**
- Put links in the post body (cuts reach ~18–50%). Add the link in the first comment or after publishing.
- Ship low-effort AI-template posts or ad-style graphics — the quality filter throttles them.
- Bury your insight in the image where crawlers and skimmers can't reach it.
- Use Unicode "bold" hacks.

---

## The 30-second pre-publish checklist
- [ ] Does the first ~140 characters contain the hook / payoff?
- [ ] Is the caption 1,300–2,500 plain-text characters, rich in specific terms?
- [ ] Is there a visual — authentic image, un-templated chart, or 8–10 slide carousel?
- [ ] Is there a reason to comment *and* a reason to save?
- [ ] Zero links in the body, zero Unicode formatting hacks?
- [ ] Is this clearly in our topic lane?
