# The Open Door at Scale — 100,000 Accounts

**Operating design for the full programme**
Plansom · *Simply done* · v1.0 · 6 October 2026

> Supersedes the volumes in the Channel Model annex. Everything else — the recipe, the offer, the control positioning, the compliance obligations — carries forward unchanged.

---

## Summary — read this first

**At 100,000 accounts this stops being a sprint and becomes an operation.** 50–79 people, 12–18 months, and a build rather than a test.

Three things you need before committing:

**🔴 The market does not exist at the current ICP.** The UK has roughly **47,000 businesses with 50+ employees** — medium and large combined. The original segment definition (£5m–£250m revenue, dedicated marketing hire) maps closely to that band. **Reaching 100,000 means either going down into the 10–49 employee band or going international.** Both change the product, the price and the economics.

**🟢 The unit economics work.** CAC lands between **£1,090 and £2,709**, with payback under nine months even at a £3,600 annual price point. This is the most encouraging number in the whole programme — and it is the first time the long-tail hypothesis has looked financially real rather than merely strategically appealing.

**🟠 Meeting capacity is still the binding constraint.** At target rates the programme generates **36,250 meetings**, needing **32 FTE** running nothing but discovery for a year.

**The recommendation is to phase it.** 100,000 is the right destination and the wrong starting point. Four waves, each gated on the last.

## 1. Does the market exist?

### What the UK actually contains

| Band | Businesses | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Large (250+ employees) | **8,335** | ONS / BEIS business population, Jan 2025 |
| Medium (50–249) | **38,435** | Same |
| **Medium + large** | **~46,770** | |
| Small (10–49) | ~215,000 | Same |
| Micro (0–9) | ~5.4 million | Same |
| **Total private sector** | **5.7 million** | |

*The published statistics are organised by employee count, not turnover. Medium + large is the closest available proxy for the original ICP of £5m–£250m revenue with a dedicated marketing function.*

### The consequence

```
Original ICP, UK only        :  ~47,000 accounts maximum
Target                       :  100,000
                                ─────────
Shortfall                    :  ~53,000
```

**You cannot get to 100,000 in the UK without changing something.** Three ways, and they are not equivalent:

| Option | Reach | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Extend down** to 10–49 employees | +215,000 available | Most have **no dedicated marketing hire**. Price point must drop sharply. Outbound calling likely stops paying for itself. Pushes toward pure self-serve |
| **B — Go international** — Ireland, Benelux, Nordics, DACH | +large pool | Language, local credibility, different compliance regimes. The UK Government credential does not travel |
| **C — Hybrid ✅ recommended** | UK 47,000 at full ICP + 53,000 from selected international markets at the same ICP | Keeps the buyer profile and price point intact. Adds market-entry complexity instead of product compromise |

> **Option C is recommended because it protects the thing that makes the hypothesis work.** The control positioning depends on the buyer having a brand worth controlling and someone employed to care about it. A 15-person business has neither. Extending down does not scale the hypothesis — it tests a different one.

### Capacity required

12-month programme, people cost only, £60k fully loaded.

| | Benchmark | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Headcount | **~50 FTE** | **~79 FTE** |
| People cost | **~£3.0m** | **~£4.7m** |
| Paid accounts | 1,098 | 4,350 |

**Payback and year-one revenue by price point:**

| Annual price | Benchmark payback | Target payback | Yr-1 revenue (bench → target) |
|---|---|---|---|
| £3,600 | 9.0 months | 3.6 months | £4.0m → £15.7m |
| £6,000 | 5.4 months | 2.2 months | £6.6m → £26.1m |
| £12,000 | 2.7 months | 1.1 months | £13.2m → £52.2m |
| £24,000 | 1.4 months | 0.5 months | £26.4m → £104.4m |

> ⚠️ **What this cost model excludes:** data and enrichment on 250,000 contacts; 54–96 Sales Navigator seats; dialler licences; the management layer above the 25% overhead; cost-to-serve on 1,000–4,350 live accounts; and churn. **Expect true cost 40–60% above the people-only figure**, and treat CAC as a floor. Even so, payback holds at every price point modelled.

### The reality check worth saying out loud

At the top end this programme delivers roughly **£104m of year-one revenue against WPP's £10.2bn** revenue less pass-through costs — about **1%**. At the bottom end, 0.04%.

**That is not a reason to reject it.** It would be the fastest-growing line in the company, built on an entirely new commercial model, in a segment WPP cannot currently serve at all. But nobody should pitch this as fixing the group's revenue problem. **Its value is proving the model, not the year-one number.**

## 3. The phased path

100,000 is the destination. Starting there would commit £3–5m and 50+ people to rates nobody has measured.

| Wave | Accounts | Timing | Purpose | Gate to proceed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Sprint** | 2,000 | Weeks 1–9 | Prove the motion. Measure real rates | ≥80 meetings, ≥8 paid, CAC <£400/meeting |
| **1 — Pilot** | 10,000 | Months 3–6 | Prove it scales past founder effort | CAC stable, cost-to-serve flat, churn <5%/mo |
| **2 — UK build** | 35,000 | Months 6–12 | Exhaust the UK ICP | Unit economics hold at 10× volume |
| **3 — International** | 53,000 | Months 12–18 | Second and third markets | Market entry validated in one before the next |
| | **100,000** | **18 months** | | |

**Each wave is funded by the last one's evidence.** Wave 0 costs weeks. Wave 3 costs millions. The gates are what make that acceptable.

## 4. Action needed

### Phase A — Foundation (months 0–3)

| # | Action | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Run Wave 0** | The 9-week sprint, unchanged. Everything below depends on its rates |
| A2 | **Resolve the market question** | Choose A, B or C from §1. This decides the product, price and hiring plan |
| A3 | **Fix the price point** | The single most consequential number. CAC works at £3,600+; below that the outbound motion stops paying |
| A4 | **Complete the compliance base** | CTPS screening contract, documented legitimate interest assessment, PECR and recording policy, DPO sign-off |
| A5 | **Build the data pipeline** | Sourcing, enrichment, net-new verification and CTPS screening as a repeatable weekly process, not a one-off list buy |
| A6 | **Design the onboarding path** | Brand live in five days, at near-zero marginal human cost. **This is the hard engineering problem** |
| A7 | **Name the programme owner** | A single accountable executive with P&L authority |

### Phase B — Build the machine (months 3–9)

| # | Action | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Hire and onboard in cohorts** | 8–10 SDRs per cohort, 6 weeks apart. Never hire a large group at once |
| B2 | **Stand up the LinkedIn estate** | Profiles warmed over 2 weeks before volume. Staggered starts |
| B3 | **Build the enablement layer** | Scripts, objection handling, qualification criteria, certification before anyone dials |
| B4 | **Instrument everything** | The attribution spine from source list to invoice, at 100× sprint volume |
| B5 | **Stand up the meeting function** | The constraint. Dedicated people, not borrowed time |
| B6 | **Build self-serve onboarding** | Every account that needs a human breaks the economics |
| B7 | **Establish the weekly operating rhythm** | Gates, bottleneck reports, cohort reviews |

### Phase C — Scale (months 9–18)

| # | Action | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Run waves 2 and 3** | Against the gates, not the calendar |
| C2 | **Enter international markets sequentially** | One at a time, each validated before the next |
| C3 | **Build the retention function** | By month 12 keeping accounts matters more than winning them |
| C4 | **Automate the long tail of the long tail** | Lowest-value accounts move to pure self-serve |
| C5 | **Continuous rate optimisation** | Every point of acceptance or connect rate is worth real headcount |

**Benchmark-rate scenario: ~50 FTE**, the difference being almost entirely meeting owners (8 instead of 32).

### Hiring reality

| Constraint | Implication |
|---|---|
| **Cohort hiring only** | 8–10 SDRs per intake, 6 weeks apart. Mass hiring destroys quality and culture simultaneously |
| **Ramp time** | 6–8 weeks to full productivity. Budget it; do not assume day-one output |
| **Attrition** | SDR attrition typically 30–40% annually. Plan to hire ~40 to hold 29 |
| **Meeting owners are not SDRs** | Different skill, different seniority, different pay. Do not try to promote into it at volume |
| **LinkedIn owners are not a hire** | 54 existing WPP people giving 20 minutes a day. **Free capacity, properly organised** |

> **The LinkedIn model is the quiet advantage.** Fifty-four WPP employees each spending twenty minutes a day delivers the entire LinkedIn channel at a cost of three FTE. No tooling, no terms-of-service risk, and higher acceptance rates than automation. It needs Plansom to assign and track 54 daily micro-tasks — which is precisely what it is for.

## 7. Tech needed

*Tech = what has to be built, as distinct from bought.*

### 7.1 The attribution spine — non-negotiable

```
Data source → Enrichment → CTPS screen → CRM → Sequencer → Dialler
     │                                     │
     └──── cohort_id · wave_id · variant_id · channel_id ────┐
                                                             ▼
                              Meeting → Trial → Activation → Paid → Support
                                                             │
                                                      Warehouse → BI
```

Every record carries its identifiers from source to invoice. **At 2,000 accounts a break is recoverable. At 100,000 it silently destroys the dataset the entire programme exists to produce.**

### 7.2 What must be built

| # | Build | Why | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **Net-new verification service** | Automated check against WPP's client master before any contact. Must run at 100k scale, repeatedly | 4–6 weeks |
| **T2** | **CTPS screening pipeline** | Automated 28-day re-screen with hard block on unscreened numbers. **Legal exposure if manual** | 3–4 weeks |
| **T3** | **Identity resolution** | One account across CRM, product, billing and support. The foundation of every cohort metric | 6–8 weeks |
| **T4** | **Warehouse and data model** | Funnel, cohort and unit economics in one place | 8–10 weeks |
| **T5** | **Self-serve onboarding — brand live in 5 days** | **The hardest and most valuable build.** Brand ingestion, guideline extraction, asset library setup, governance rules — with minimal human touch | 12–16 weeks |
| **T6** | **AI personalisation layer** | 250,000 personalised invite notes and call-prep briefs. Impossible manually; the acceptance-rate difference between generic and personalised is roughly 2× | 6–8 weeks |
| **T7** | **Real-time operations dashboard** | 79 people against weekly gates. Daily refresh is too slow at this scale | 4–6 weeks |
| **T8** | **Plansom task orchestration** | ~500 recurring daily tasks across 79 people, auto-assigned, auto-tracked, with bottleneck detection | Configuration |
| **T9** | **Brand governance engine** | The product moat. Brand rules enforced automatically on every asset produced | Product roadmap |

### 7.3 The two that decide the programme

**T5 — self-serve onboarding.** Every account needing human setup costs 2–4 hours. At 4,350 accounts that is 8,700–17,400 hours, or 5–10 additional FTE, and it scales linearly forever. **If onboarding cannot be automated, tail economics fail regardless of how good the outreach is.**

**T9 — brand governance engine.** This is what the whole hypothesis is sold on. Control is the wedge. If the product cannot actually enforce brand rules automatically, the positioning is a promise rather than a feature, and competitors with better generation models win on everything else.

> **Both sit on WPP's product roadmap, not in the go-to-market programme.** They are dependencies this operation cannot deliver for itself, and they should be confirmed as funded and dated before Wave 2. **Running a 100,000-account outbound programme against a product that cannot onboard or govern at scale would be the most expensive way to learn that lesson.**

### 7.4 AI inside the operation

| Application | Effect |
|---|---|
| Personalised invite notes at 250k scale | ~2× acceptance vs generic |
| Call-prep briefs per contact | Higher connect-to-meeting conversion |
| Transcript coding and theme extraction | Qualitative insight at 36,250 meetings — impossible manually |
| Call scoring and coaching | Consistency across 29 SDRs |
| Lead scoring and prioritisation | Work the best accounts first |
| Automated standups and bottleneck reports | Plansom AI Manage across 79 people |

## 8. Risks at this scale

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Market smaller than 100,000 at current ICP** | **Critical** | Resolve §1 before Wave 2. Hybrid option recommended |
| **Price point below £3,600** | **Critical** | Outbound stops paying. Fix price in Phase A |
| **T5 onboarding not automated** | **Critical** | Confirm product roadmap funding before Wave 2 |
| **LinkedIn restricts at estate scale** | High | 100/seat ceiling, staggered starts, manual sending, warm-up |
| **CTPS breach** | High | Automated pipeline with hard block. Never manual |
| **Meeting capacity under-provisioned** | High | 32 FTE at target rates. Throttle top of funnel rather than rush |
| **SDR attrition above 40%** | High | Cohort hiring, continuous pipeline, realistic ramp budgeting |
| **Brand damage at volume** | High | Brand governance seat with authority to stop the programme |
| **Attribution breaks at scale** | High | T1–T4 complete before Wave 2 |
| **Cannibalisation becomes visible** | Medium | Net-new verification is the control. Monitor the rate continuously |
| **Agency network resistance** | Medium | Executive sponsorship. Publish results, not plans |

## 10. The honest recommendation

**Commit to 100,000 as the destination. Do not commit to it as the plan.**

The economics are genuinely encouraging — CAC of £1,090–£2,709 with payback under nine months at every price point modelled is a real business, and the first hard evidence that the long-tail hypothesis is financially viable rather than just strategically attractive.

But three things are unresolved and all three are material: **the market does not exist at the stated ICP**, the **price point is unfixed**, and **two critical product capabilities are not confirmed as funded**.

Run Wave 0. It costs nine weeks and answers the rate question that every number in this document is sized from. Then Wave 1 at 10,000 proves the motion survives contact with scale. By month six you will know whether 100,000 is a plan or an ambition — and you will have spent a fraction of what committing today would cost.

> **The sprint is not a smaller version of this programme. It is the thing that tells you whether to build it.**
