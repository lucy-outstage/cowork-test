## 1. Executive summary

**WPP is not a declining business. It is a stabilising business carrying the reputation of a declining one.**

That distinction is the single most important output of this diagnosis, because it changes what the sprint should work on.

The headline numbers invite the pessimistic reading. Full-year 2025 revenue less pass-through costs fell 5.4% like-for-like to £10,176m. Headline operating margin fell from 15.0% to 13.0%. Two profit warnings in 2025 took roughly 62% off the share price. WPP left the FTSE 100 in December 2025 after almost three decades. Headcount is down around 9,000 year on year. [S1, S2, S9, S13]

The quarterly data tells a different story. WPP's like-for-like decline in revenue less pass-through costs went from **−6.7% in Q1 2026 to −2.8% in Q2 2026** — a 3.9 percentage point improvement in three months. Client retention rose from **16% to 43%** year on year. Lost account value fell from roughly **$6.98bn in 2025 to $2.81bn in 2026**, against more than **$3bn won**. Margin rose 0.2 points like-for-like while revenue was still falling. And on **1 October 2026 WPP re-entered the FTSE 100**. [S4, S5, S7, S14, S15]

Underneath that, a figure WPP does not publish but which can be derived from what it does: **revenue less pass-through costs per employee rose approximately 3.2%** year on year, from roughly £47,400 to £48,900 per half. WPP is generating more value per person than it did a year ago. That is the strongest available public evidence that the AI investment and the restructuring are producing a real operational effect rather than only a cost effect. [Appendix B.1]

**Five findings follow from this.**

**One — the recovery is real but young.** It rests on two quarters and a retention statistic. The Q3 trading update on 29 October 2026 is the first genuine test. No conclusion drawn here should be locked before it lands.

**Two — WPP Open is proven internally and unproven commercially.** More than 75,000 internal users and over 90% of client-facing staff is substantial adoption. [S10] But there is no published Open revenue, no ARR, no paid-client count and no contribution margin, and there will not be until Enterprise Solutions is separately disclosed in 2027. [S3] This is a measurement gap, not evidence of weakness — but it means no external party can currently assess whether Open makes money.

**Three — adoption does not protect the relationship.** Coca-Cola is a named WPP Open client and also moved its North American media business to Publicis. [S10, S12] WPP has a worked example proving that platform adoption and assignment retention are separate outcomes. Any strategy treating Open as a retention mechanism must account for this case.

**Four — the efficiency gain is at risk of being given away.** Open's demonstrated benefits are time-based: 14 hours saved per week for a team of four, four weeks of strategy compressed into three hours, 33 times the asset volume. [S11, S21] On contracts that bill for time, every hour saved is revenue forgone. The JLR mandate — global integrated creative and media with **outcome-based remuneration** — is the structural answer. [S16] Outcome pricing is what converts efficiency into retained margin rather than lower fees. This is, in our view, the most important commercial question in front of the business.

**Five — the moat cannot be the technology.** Open Pro's generation runs on Google Imagen and Veo, OpenAI DALL-E and Sora, Adobe Firefly, with text from Gemini, Claude and ChatGPT, and WPP has committed $400m to Google over five years. [S17, S18] Those models are available to everyone, including Google's and Canva's own competing marketing tools. [S19] What WPP alone owns is proprietary audience and brand data, brand governance and safety, and accountability for outcomes. That is the answer to protecting the brand while scaling Open: **Open's defensible value is brand control, not content generation.**

**The reframed sprint question.** The risk is not that WPP Open fails. The risk is that it succeeds — making WPP materially more efficient under commercial models that pay for time, so the gain passes to clients as lower fees instead of to WPP as margin. The sprint's work is therefore commercial-model design, not product adoption.

## 2. Premise and scope

### 2.1 The brief as given

The sprint proceeds from three stated needs:

1. **Satisfy customers** — through WPP Open and better work production delivery.
2. **Streamline and simplify** the way the business works.
3. **Make the most of WPP Open** without watering down the brand.

### 2.2 What this addendum does

It tests those three premises against the public record, and establishes:

- Whether the needs as stated match what the evidence shows.
- What is genuinely known, what is company-reported, and what is unknowable from outside.
- Where the highest-value questions sit for discovery.

### 2.3 What changed from the initial discovery notes

The initial discovery work was sound in structure and conservative in its claims — notably in refusing to infer average contract value or client tenure from disclosures that do not support it. That caution is preserved here.

Three substantive changes were made:

| Change | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Period labelling corrected** | Divisional figures were presented beside full-year 2025 revenue but are H1 2026. Compounding this, FY2025 revenue less pass-through costs fell 5.4% *and* WPP Media's H1 2026 revenue fell 5.4% — the same number for two unrelated things |
| **Per-client calculation replaced** | Dividing total revenue by 1,700 disclosed clients produced ~£6m. Because the top 100 take roughly half of revenue, the long tail averages **≤£3.2m**. The original figure pointed the wrong way (Appendix B.2) |
| **Six material facts added** | Quarterly trajectory, client retention, account flow, the UK Government win, FTSE 100 re-entry, and net debt. Each is load-bearing |

### 2.4 Boundaries

This diagnosis does **not** cover: WPP's creative output quality; individual agency brand health below divisional level; talent, culture or employee sentiment beyond headcount; legal or regulatory exposure; detailed competitor financial analysis beyond growth-rate comparison; or any assessment of Open's technical architecture. These are out of scope, not judged immaterial.

## 3. Method and evidence standard

### 3.1 Approach

1. **Re-test every claim** in the initial discovery notes against the primary record (Appendix A).
2. **Separate reported from derived.** Anything calculated here is shown with workings (Appendix B).
3. **Grade confidence** on every material figure (Appendix D).
4. **Record what could not be established**, rather than filling gaps with inference.
5. **Reject contradicted sources** explicitly rather than silently.

### 3.2 Evidence hierarchy

| Tier | Description | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Primary** | WPP RNS announcements, Annual Report and Accounts 2025 (Form 20-F), interim results statements | Treated as authoritative |
| **Tier 2 — Corroborated secondary** | Trade and financial press reporting a Tier 1 figure, where two or more independent outlets agree | Treated as reliable |
| **Tier 3 — Single secondary** | One credible outlet, uncorroborated | Marked in-line |
| **Tier 4 — Company-reported claim** | WPP marketing material and case studies | Reported as claims, never as verified outcomes |

### 3.3 A material limitation, stated plainly

The research environment used for this work could not open PDFs or investor-relations pages directly. Outbound requests to `wpp.com`, `sec.gov`, `investegate.co.uk` and most publisher domains were blocked by a network proxy.

**Everything in this report was therefore verified through search against named primary documents rather than by reading those documents directly.** Where two or more independent sources report the same Tier 1 figure, confidence is high. Where only one does, it is marked.

**Recommendation:** before this analysis is used in a formal or client-facing setting, the four primary documents listed in Appendix C.1 should be pulled directly and the Tier 1 figures confirmed. We estimate this at under two hours of work. Nothing in our findings is expected to change, but the sourcing chain would then be complete.

### 3.4 One source rejected

A search result claimed WPP outperformed the FTSE 100 over three and five years (+47.8% and +61.2% respectively). This is **irreconcilable** with the documented ~62% share price fall during 2025 and the consequent demotion from the index. It appears to be a mis-parse of comparative index data. **It is not used anywhere in this report.** It is recorded here because a reader encountering it elsewhere should know it was considered and discarded.

## 5. Findings

Twelve findings, each with the evidence, and what we read it to mean.

---

### F1 — The decline is decelerating sharply, and half-year reporting conceals it

**Evidence.** Like-for-like revenue less pass-through costs: FY2025 −5.4%; Q1 2026 −6.7%; Q2 2026 −2.8%; H1 2026 blended −4.7%. [S1, S4, S5]

**Analysis.** The H1 figure of −4.7% is the average of a poor quarter and a markedly better one. Reported alone, it describes a business that no longer exists. Between January and June 2026 the rate of decline **more than halved** — a 3.9 point improvement in a single quarter.

**What it means.** Any diagnosis built on half-year or full-year averages will systematically understate the turn. Guidance of low-to-mid single-digit decline for H2 2026 implies management expects stabilisation rather than continued improvement — worth noting as a conservative posture. [S5]

**Confidence: High.** Corroborated across multiple outlets citing the same results statement.

---

### F2 — Margin held through a revenue decline, which is harder than it sounds

**Evidence.** H1 2026 headline operating margin 8.4%, **up 0.2 points like-for-like**, on revenue less pass-through costs down 4.7%. FY2025 margin 13.0% (down from 15.0%). FY2026 guidance 12–13%. [S1, S4, S5]

**Analysis.** Improving margin while revenue falls means cost reduction is running **ahead of** revenue loss, not chasing it. WPP attributes the improvement to cost savings and lower headline severance, partly offset by growth investment and incentives. [S22]

**What it means.** The two-point annual margin slide of 2025 has become approximately flat. The cost programme is landing. The constraint now is revenue, not efficiency.

**Confidence: High.**

---

### F3 — Revenue per employee is rising *(calculated — not published by WPP)*

**Evidence and workings.**

| | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue less pass-through costs | ~£5,026m *(derived)* | £4,745m | −5.6% |
| Average headcount | ~106,000 | ~97,000 | −8.4% |
| **RLPTC per head, per half** | **~£47,400** | **~£48,900** | **+3.2%** |

*Full workings at Appendix B.1. H1 2025 RLPTC is derived from the reported −5.6% decline; headcount is WPP's average rather than period-end; portfolio disposals affect both lines. Directional, not audited.*

**Analysis.** WPP is producing more revenue per person than a year ago while shrinking. Headcount fell faster (−8.4%) than revenue (−5.6%), and the gap is the productivity gain.

**What it means.** This is the closest thing in the public record to evidence that the AI investment and restructuring are producing an operational effect, not merely a cost effect. It is also the metric most directly connected to the WPP Open thesis — and WPP does not publish it.

**Confidence: Calculated.** Methodologically sound but sensitive to the headcount basis and to disposals. Should be reproduced internally with actual figures before being relied upon externally.

---

### F4 — Client retention has recovered sharply

**Evidence.** Retention 16% → **43%** year on year. Renewals include Huawei, Tesco and L'Oréal, supported by a new client success programme. [S7]

**Analysis.** WPP's 2025 problem was never an inability to win work — it was an inability to keep it. Retention is a leading indicator: revenue follows account flow by roughly twelve to eighteen months.

**What it means.** This is the mechanism behind the F1 trajectory, and it explains why guidance points at growth during **2027** rather than 2026. The revenue effect of 2026 retention has not yet appeared in revenue.

**Confidence: Medium.** Reported by a credible analyst outlet; we could not locate the underlying definition of "retention rate" in a primary document. **The definition should be confirmed** — retention measured by account count, billings, or revenue would give materially different pictures.

---

### F5 — Account flow has turned from heavily negative to marginally positive

**Evidence.** 2025 lost account value ~$6.98bn. 2026: >$3bn won against $2.81bn lost. WPP Media secured $5.2bn in combined incremental and retained billings in H1 2026 — the highest of any major media group, with retentions nearly 40% of the total and ~$600m ahead of its nearest competitor. [S7, S24]

**Analysis.** The swing is roughly **$4.2bn** in reduced losses year on year. WPP Media topped the COMvergence H1 2026 new business rankings. [S24]

**What it means.** The bleeding stopped. But net flow is only marginally positive — WPP is roughly treading water on account value, not yet accumulating. That is consistent with stabilisation in 2026 and growth in 2027, and inconsistent with a faster recovery.

**Confidence: Medium.** Billings values are industry-tracked estimates, not audited. Directionally robust; precise values should not be quoted as fact.

---

### F6 — The UK Government win validates simplification as a *proposition*, not just a programme

**Evidence.** WPP Media won the UK Government media account — approximately **£1.5bn over four years** — from Omnicom's MG OMD. The government's stated rationale included reducing suppliers from **33 to 23**, cutting campaign planning times and management costs, and streamlining cross-government messaging. [S25]

**Analysis.** This is WPP's largest single win in the period, and it was won on a consolidation and simplification argument.

**What it means.** "Streamline and simplify" is not only an internal efficiency objective. It is something clients are actively buying, and WPP has just proven it can win on that basis. The internal programme and the external proposition are the same argument. **This is the strongest validation of sprint need 2.**

Note also the counter-intuitive geography: the UK was WPP's **weakest** major region in 2025 at −7.6% like-for-like. [S20] The largest win came in the weakest market.

**Confidence: High** on the award; **Medium** on the precise value (reported variously as £1.5bn and ~$2bn).

---

### F7 — Production is the only growing division, and it is the smallest

**Evidence.** WPP Production: **+1.6%** LFL in H1 2026 (Q2: +1.3%), against Media −5.4% and Creative −4.9%. Production is **5%** of revenue less pass-through costs — approximately £237m in H1 2026. [S4]

**Analysis.** Production is also where WPP Open's benefits are most measurable: the Hawkstone case, and the Google partnership's claim of up to 70% faster production with doubled asset utilisation. [S18, S21]

**What it means.** WPP's fastest-growing capability is its smallest, and it is the one where the AI investment demonstrably works. A division at 5% of revenue cannot carry group growth on its own — but it is the clearest proof point available that the Open thesis translates into client-visible delivery improvement.

**Confidence: High.**

---

### F8 — WPP Open's internal adoption is substantial; its commercial performance is unmeasurable from outside

**Evidence — established.** 75,000+ internal users; >90% of client-facing staff; clients including Nestlé and Coca-Cola; ~£300m invested in AI, data and Open in 2025 (2024: £250m); $400m five-year Google commitment; Open Pro launched 24 October 2025 on an access fee plus usage-based model. [S10, S17, S18, S26, S27]

**Evidence — not established.** No separate Open revenue. No ARR. No paid-client count. No contribution margin. No public price list. No disclosed lifetime build cost. [S3, S27]

**Analysis.** Open is established as **delivery infrastructure**. Its direct commercial offering is less than a year old. Enterprise Solutions — where Open's commercial contribution would appear — is reported inside Creative until separate disclosure begins in 2027. [S4]

**What it means.** The absence of Open financials is a **reporting-structure consequence, not evidence of weakness**. But it also means no external party can currently assess whether Open makes money, and internal measurement is the only route to an answer.

**Confidence: High** on adoption figures as company-reported; **High** on the absence of disclosure.

---

### F9 — Platform adoption does not protect the client relationship

**Evidence.** Coca-Cola is a named WPP Open client. [S10] Coca-Cola also moved its North American media business to Publicis during 2025. [S12]

**Analysis.** Platform adoption and assignment retention are demonstrably separate outcomes. WPP has a worked example in its own client base.

**What it means.** Any strategy or investment case treating Open as a **retention** mechanism must explain this case before proceeding. Open may well improve delivery, satisfaction and economics — but the evidence that it holds accounts is, at present, contradicted by WPP's own largest-profile example.

This was the sharpest observation in the initial discovery notes and it survives scrutiny intact.

**Confidence: High** on both facts; **the inference is ours.**

---

### F10 — Open Pro is built on the technology of its own competitors

**Evidence.** Open Pro's image and video generation uses Google Imagen and Veo, OpenAI DALL-E and Sora, and Adobe Firefly; text generation uses Google Gemini, Anthropic Claude and OpenAI ChatGPT. WPP has committed $400m to Google technologies over five years. Trade analysis notes Open Pro competing with Google's and Canva's own marketing tools. [S17, S18, S19]

**Analysis.** WPP pays for the model layer from vendors who also compete in the application layer. The generation capability is therefore, by definition, not proprietary — it is available to any competitor, and to WPP's own clients directly.

**What it means.** **The moat cannot be the technology.** What WPP alone owns is proprietary audience and brand data, brand governance and safety frameworks, integration across media, creative and production, and accountability for outcomes.

This is the direct answer to sprint need 3. Open's defensible value — the thing that does not water down the brand — is **brand control, not content generation.**

**Confidence: High** on the technology stack; **the strategic inference is ours.**

---

### F11 — Open Pro's target market overlaps WPP's own client tail

**Evidence.** Open Pro is positioned for marketers who want to plan, create and publish *"without the help of an agency"*, and for clients who *"cannot afford a full-service integrated agency"* — startups, regional teams, smaller businesses. [S26, S27] WPP's own tail comprises roughly 1,600 clients outside the top 100, averaging **≤£3.2m**, on contracts commonly cancellable at 90 days. [S2, Appendix B.2] Management states it expects Open Pro revenue to be incremental, with managed-service pull-through also incremental. [S27]

**Analysis.** The stated intention is incrementality. That intention is reasonable and may well prove correct. But it is currently an expectation, not a measured outcome, and the segment descriptions overlap materially.

**What it means.** The question **"is Open Pro revenue incremental or substitutional?"** is answerable today from WPP's own billing data, and it determines whether Open Pro growth is value creation or value transfer. It should be the first thing the sprint establishes.

**Confidence: High** on positioning and client structure; **Medium** on the overlap inference — it is a reasonable reading of segment descriptions, not a measured finding.

---

### F12 — The balance sheet limits optionality

**Evidence.** Net debt £2,935m against market capitalisation of ~£4.1bn — roughly **72p of net debt for every £1 of equity value**. Restructuring costs of ~£400m over two years, ~£190m in 2026. Interim dividend maintained at 7.5p. Adjusted operating cash flow before working capital £309m in H1 2026, including £83m of non-headline cash restructuring. [S6, S9, S22]

**Analysis.** WPP is funding a major restructuring, a significant technology commitment and a maintained dividend simultaneously, from a leveraged position.

**What it means.** There is limited room to spend the company's way out of a sequencing problem. This raises the stakes on getting the Open commercial model right first time, and makes the margin-retention question in F10 and the incrementality question in F11 financially consequential rather than merely analytically interesting.

**Confidence: High** on figures; **the interpretation is ours.**

## 6. The analysis

This section sets out the reasoning that connects the findings, rather than restating them.

### 6.1 The central chain

The findings are not twelve independent observations. They form one causal chain:

```
   Complexity and inconsistent execution  (Rose's own diagnosis, F-context)
                    ↓
        Client losses accelerate in 2025   ($6.98bn lost — F5)
                    ↓
   Revenue falls, margin compresses 15% → 13%   (F2)
                    ↓
   Elevate28: simplify, integrate, cut £500m    (§4.6)
                    ↓
        Retention recovers 16% → 43%            (F4)
                    ↓
     Account flow turns net positive            (F5)
                    ↓
   Rate of revenue decline halves Q1 → Q2       (F1)
                    ↓
   Margin rises while revenue still falls       (F2)
                    ↓
  Revenue per head rises +3.2%                  (F3)
                    ↓
        → Return to organic growth during 2027   (guidance)
```

**The chain is coherent and the observable links are in the right order.** Retention improved before account flow turned; account flow turned before the revenue decline slowed; the decline slowed while margin improved. That sequence is what a genuine operational recovery looks like, and it is difficult to fake.

**The weakest link is the last one.** Everything up to and including F3 is observed. The return to growth in 2027 is guidance.

### 6.2 Why half-year reporting misleads here

This deserves isolating because it is the most common analytical error available with this dataset.

WPP's reported H1 2026 figure is −4.7%. A reader comparing it to FY2025's −5.4% concludes the business improved by 0.7 points in eighteen months — essentially flat, essentially still broken.

The quarterly data says the business improved by **3.9 points in three months**. [S4, S5]

Both statements are true. Only the second is useful. Reporting cadence, not business performance, creates the discrepancy — and the sprint should work from the quarterly series throughout.

The same caution applies to margin. H1 margin of 8.4% against a full-year 13.0% looks like collapse and is in fact seasonality. The like-for-like H1-on-H1 comparison (+0.2pp) is the only valid read. [S4]

### 6.3 The efficiency paradox

This is, in our view, the most consequential piece of reasoning in the diagnosis.

Every published WPP Open benefit is expressed as **time saved**:

| Claim | Unit |
|---|---|
| 14 hours per week returned to a four-person team (≈90 days of annual capacity) | Time |
| Strategy and creative development: four weeks → three hours | Time |
| Hawkstone Harvest IPA: 33× asset volume from a four-person team in two days | Time and volume |
| Google model access: up to 70% faster production, doubled asset utilisation | Time |

*Sources: S11, S18, S21. All company-reported.*

Under a commercial model that bills for time — retainers scaled to headcount, project fees estimated from hours, FTE-based pricing — **time saved is revenue destroyed.** The more effective Open becomes, the faster the fee base erodes. The efficiency accrues to the client, and WPP's reward for building the platform is a smaller invoice.

This is not hypothetical. It is the structural reason agency groups have historically been cautious about automation, and it is the mechanism by which a successful platform can shrink the business that built it.

**There are only three escapes:**

1. **Outcome-based pricing** — WPP is paid for results, so efficiency gains are retained as margin. The JLR mandate is explicitly structured this way. [S16]
2. **Volume expansion** — the same fee buys 33× the assets, and clients buy more because the unit economics changed. The Hawkstone case gestures at this.
3. **New revenue entirely** — Open Pro reaching buyers who were never going to retain an agency, which is management's stated expectation. [S27]

**All three are live at WPP. None is yet proven at scale.** This is where the sprint creates the most value.

### 6.4 Why the moat question and the brand question are the same question

Sprint need 3 asks how to maximise Open without watering down the brand. The usual framing treats this as a tension between reach and prestige — sell to more people, dilute the name.

F10 suggests a cleaner resolution.

WPP does not own the generation capability. It licenses it from Google, OpenAI and Adobe, who also sell competing products. [S17, S19] If WPP's brand promise were "we make content," the brand is already commoditised and the dilution has already happened — not by Open Pro, but by the models being available to everyone including WPP's clients.

What WPP owns that others do not: proprietary audience and brand data; brand governance and safety at scale; integration across media, creative and production; and accountability for commercial outcomes.

That reframes the dividing line into something testable:

| | Sells | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| **Open Pro** | Brand-safe execution capacity | Buyers who were never going to retain an agency |
| **WPP managed services** | Judgement, integration, accountability for outcomes | Buyers who need them |

On this reading, Open Pro does not dilute the brand — **provided it is sold as governed execution rather than as cheap creative.** The dilution risk sits in positioning and segmentation, not in the existence of the product.

### 6.5 The concentration asymmetry

A top-ten client is worth roughly **67 times** a client in the long tail (£214m against ≤£3.2m — Appendix B.2).

Two consequences pull in opposite directions, and the sprint must hold both:

- **Defensively**, concentration is the dominant commercial risk. Losing one top-ten relationship costs more than losing several hundred tail clients. The 2025 Coca-Cola and Mars media losses are the demonstration — and both were in CPG, WPP's largest sector exposure at 28% of revenue and the sector where WPP itself identified the sharpest client spending cuts. [S20]
- **Offensively**, the tail is Open Pro's natural market and WPP already serves it. Roughly 1,600 clients at ≤£3.2m average, on 90-day terms. WPP does not need to find this segment.

**The hazard is that these are the same clients.** A tail client on a 90-day contract, offered a self-serve tool at a fraction of a retainer, has an obvious and immediate option. This is F11 restated as a commercial risk rather than an analytical question.

### 6.6 What the competitive position actually demands

Publicis grew **+5.5%** organically in 2025 while WPP fell **−4.8%** — a gap of more than ten points. [S8] Omnicom and Interpublic have merged. [S8]

WPP cannot close a ten-point gap through cost reduction. Cost work protects margin; it does not win accounts. The gap closes only through retention (F4, now moving) and through propositions clients prefer (F6, demonstrated once at scale with the UK Government).

**The strategic implication:** Elevate28's cost programme is necessary but is not the growth engine. The growth engine has to be integrated, simplified, outcome-accountable delivery — which is precisely what the UK Government bought and what the JLR mandate is structured around. Those two accounts are the template, and the sprint should treat them as such.

## 8. The three needs, assessed

### Need 1 — Satisfy customers: WPP Open and better work production delivery

**Verdict: ✅ Valid, and partly already achieved — but under-exploited.**

The customer satisfaction metric that matters most has already moved: retention 16% → 43%, with Huawei, Tesco and L'Oréal renewed. [S7] That is the clearest available evidence that delivery is improving, and it is a leading indicator rather than a lagging one.

Production — the division most associated with Open's measurable benefits — is the **only growing division** at +1.6% LFL. [S4] But it is 5% of revenue less pass-through costs.

> **The diagnosis:** WPP's fastest-growing capability is its smallest, and it is the one where its AI investment demonstrably works. The gap between "Open works in production" and "Open drives group revenue" is a scaling problem, not a proof problem.

**What the sprint should do:** stop trying to prove Open delivers value. That is established. Work instead on why a capability that works at 5% of the business has not propagated to the 95%.

---

### Need 2 — Streamline and simplify the way the business works

**Verdict: ✅ Valid, leadership-endorsed, and externally validated by buyers.**

Rose named complexity as the root cause of underperformance. [S3] Elevate28 commits to a single-company structure, four divisions, four regions, and £500m of gross annualised savings. [S3, S22] At 4.9% of FY2025 revenue less pass-through costs, that is a material margin opportunity if retained (Appendix B.4).

The external validation is stronger than the internal commitment. **The UK Government bought simplification explicitly** — 33 suppliers reduced to 23, faster planning, lower management cost — and awarded WPP roughly £1.5bn over four years on that basis. [S25]

> **The diagnosis:** simplification is not only an efficiency programme. It is a proposition clients are purchasing, and WPP has proven it can win on it at the largest scale.

**The risk is execution load.** WPP is simultaneously restructuring four divisions into four regions, removing £500m of cost, absorbing ~9,000 fewer people year on year, replacing the CEO of its largest creative network, and commercialising a new platform — while Publicis compounds growth and the merged Omnicom–IPG integrates. Simplification programmes consume the capacity needed to deliver the client work that proves them.

**What the sprint should do:** treat simplification as a sellable proposition, not only an internal programme. The UK Government win is a repeatable template and should be productised.

---

### Need 3 — Make the most of WPP Open without watering down the brand

**Verdict: ⚠️ Valid, but the tension is structural and resolves through segmentation and pricing, not through volume.**

Open Pro's own positioning — campaigns *without an agency*, for clients who *cannot afford* one — is a disintermediation product sold by an intermediary. [S26, S27] Pointed at the wrong segment it cannibalises (P2). Pointed at the right one it extends reach into revenue WPP could not otherwise serve.

The resolution follows from §6.4: WPP's brand is not its ability to make content — generation is commoditised and rented from vendors who compete with WPP. [S17, S19] WPP's brand is its ability to make content that is **strategically right and safe to publish at scale**.

> **The diagnosis:** the brand dilution risk lives in positioning and pricing, not in the product's existence. Open Pro sold as *governed, brand-safe execution capacity* extends the brand. Sold as *cheap creative*, it commoditises it.

**The mechanism that makes this work is outcome-based pricing.** The JLR mandate — global integrated creative and media with outcome-based remuneration — is the structural answer to §6.3. [S16] When WPP is paid for outcomes rather than time, Open stops being a device that deflates fees and becomes margin WPP retains.

**What the sprint should do:** treat JLR as a pricing template to generalise, and define the Open Pro / managed services segmentation line as an explicit commercial policy before volume forces the issue.

## 10. Hypothesis and discovery brief

### 10.1 The original hypothesis

> *WPP Open has demonstrated adoption and delivery benefits. The commercial opportunity is to turn those benefits into a simple offer that a defined customer segment understands, buys and uses profitably.*

This is sound and should be retained. Its limitation is an implicit assumption that WPP's core problem is still revenue decline. On the evidence in F1–F5, the decline is already being addressed by mechanisms now visible in the data.

### 10.2 The revised hypothesis

> **WPP has proven WPP Open works internally — 75,000 users, rising revenue per head, margin held through a revenue decline. It has not proven Open creates value it can keep.**
>
> **The risk is not that Open fails. The risk is that Open succeeds — making WPP materially more efficient under commercial models that pay for time, so the gain passes to clients as lower fees rather than to WPP as margin.**
>
> **The sprint's work is therefore commercial-model design, not product adoption.**

### 10.3 The first decision

Which offer to examine: **Open Pro for independent client use**, or **Open-enabled managed service**. Their buyers, economics and buying journeys differ.

**Our recommendation: examine them together.** F11 and §6.5 establish that the risk lives in the gap between them — specifically in whether Open Pro reaches new money or re-prices existing money. Examining either in isolation cannot answer that.

### 10.4 Questions to test

| # | Question | Why it matters | Where the answer is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Which customers pay for Open today, and under what commercial arrangement? | Establishes the real revenue model | Internal billing — available now |
| Q2 | **Is Open Pro revenue incremental or substitutional?** | Decides whether growth is real (F11) | Client-level revenue history, pre/post adoption |
| Q3 | **Does Open-enabled delivery raise or lower effective fee rates?** | Tests whether efficiency is accretive or dilutive (§6.3) | Margin per hour: Open-delivered vs traditional accounts |
| Q4 | Which use case most consistently produces a result clients value? | Identifies a credible, repeatable offer | Pilot data behind the 14-hour and 33× claims |
| Q5 | Where do qualified buyers stop progressing? | Locates the acquisition obstacle | Open Pro funnel since 24 October 2025 |
| Q6 | What do onboarding and ongoing support cost per account? | Tests whether tail growth is profitable | Cost-to-serve on the Open Pro cohort |
| Q7 | Can outcome-based pricing extend beyond JLR? | The margin-retention mechanism (§6.3) | JLR contract structure as template |
| Q8 | Does Open generate incremental revenue, retain services, or replace existing fees? | Establishes net contribution to WPP | Synthesis of Q2, Q3 and Q6 |

**Q2, Q3 and Q7 are the critical path.** Q1, Q4, Q5 and Q6 are supporting evidence. Q8 is the synthesis.

## Appendix B — Calculation workings

Every derived figure in this report, with its method and limitations.

### B.1 Revenue less pass-through costs per employee

**Purpose:** test whether productivity improved, since WPP does not publish this.

```
H1 2026 RLPTC                     = £4,745m                        [S4]
H1 2026 reported decline          = −5.6%                          [S4]

H1 2025 RLPTC (derived)           = £4,745m ÷ (1 − 0.056)
                                  = £4,745m ÷ 0.944
                                  = £5,026.5m

Average headcount H1 2025         = ~106,000                       [S4]
Average headcount H1 2026         = ~97,000                        [S4]

RLPTC per head, H1 2025           = £5,026.5m ÷ 106,000 = £47,420
RLPTC per head, H1 2026           = £4,745.0m ÷  97,000 = £48,918

Change                            = (48,918 ÷ 47,420) − 1
                                  = +3.16%  →  ~+3.2%
```

**Why it works:** headcount fell faster (−8.4%) than revenue (−5.6%). The gap is the productivity gain.

**Limitations.** H1 2025 RLPTC is derived from a rounded percentage, not reported directly. Headcount is WPP's *average*, not period-end. Portfolio disposals reduce both numerator and denominator and are not isolated here. **Directional, not audited** — should be reproduced internally with actual figures.

### B.2 Revenue per client by band

**Purpose:** replace the misleading "~£6m per client" figure.

```
FY2025 RLPTC                      = £10,176m                       [S1]
Top 10 clients  = 21% of RLPTC    = £2,136.96m  ÷ 10  = £213.7m    [S2]
Top 100 clients = ~50% of RLPTC   = £5,088.00m  ÷ 100 = £50.9m     [S2]

Clients 101–~1,700 (≈1,600 clients) share AT MOST the remaining
£5,088m — and in practice less, because all clients below the
£500k disclosure threshold also draw on that same pool.

Upper bound per tail client       = £5,088m ÷ 1,600 = £3.18m

Concentration ratio               = £213.7m ÷ £3.18m = 67.2×
```

**Why the original figure misleads.** £10,176m ÷ 1,700 = £5.99m is arithmetically correct but places the numerator (all revenue, including thousands of sub-£500k clients) over a denominator that excludes them. It implies a typical disclosed client is worth ~£6m when the top-heavy distribution means the typical one is worth **at most half that**.

### B.3 Net debt relative to equity value

```
Net debt (H1 2026)                = £2,935m                        [S6]
Market capitalisation (Oct 2026)  = ~£4,102m                       [S9]
Ratio                             = 2,935 ÷ 4,102 = 71.5%
                                  → ~72p net debt per £1 equity value
```

*Market capitalisation is a point-in-time figure and moves daily. Indicative of constraint, not a formal leverage measure (which would use EBITDA).*

### B.4 Cost savings in context

```
Elevate28 gross annualised savings = £500m                         [S3, S22]
FY2025 RLPTC                       = £10,176m                      [S1]
Savings as % of RLPTC              = 500 ÷ 10,176 = 4.91%

Restructuring cost                 = ~£400m over two years         [S22]
Payback at full run-rate           = 400 ÷ 500 = 0.8 years
```

*The payback figure assumes savings are fully retained rather than competed away in pricing — which §6.3 argues is precisely the open question.*

### B.5 Divisional absolute sizes, H1 2026

```
H1 2026 RLPTC                     = £4,745m                        [S4]
WPP Media     46%                 = £2,182.7m
WPP Creative  49%                 = £2,325.1m
WPP Production 5%                 = £  237.3m
```

*Production annualises to roughly £475m — about 4.7% of FY2025 group RLPTC. The only growing division is roughly one twentieth of the business (F7).*

### B.6 Rate-of-change figures

```
Q1 2026 LFL  −6.7%  →  Q2 2026 LFL  −2.8%     [S4, S5]
Improvement  = 3.9 percentage points in one quarter

Retention    16% → 43%                         [S7]
```

## Appendix D — Confidence framework and limitations

### D.1 Confidence ratings applied

| Rating | Meaning | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | Two or more independent sources traceable to a named WPP primary document | FY2025 and H1 2026 financials; Elevate28 structure and targets; divisional splits; geographic and sector mix; client concentration; leadership changes; Open Pro launch and model stack; Google commitment; FTSE 100 movements |
| **Medium** | Single credible source, or industry-tracked estimate | Client retention 16%→43%; new-business won/lost values; UK Government contract value; share price and market capitalisation |
| **Company-reported** | WPP's own claim, not independently verified | 75,000+ users; >90% client-facing adoption; all three productivity claims; Nestlé and Coca-Cola as Open clients |
| **Calculated** | Derived in this report; workings in Appendix B | RLPTC per head (+3.2%); revenue per client band; concentration ratio 67×; net debt to market cap; savings as % of RLPTC; divisional absolute sizes |
| **Inference** | Our reading, explicitly flagged | F9's conclusion on adoption vs retention; F10's moat argument; F11's overlap; §6.3's efficiency paradox; §6.4's brand resolution |

### D.2 What we could not establish

Recorded as gaps rather than filled with inference:

| Unknown | Why it matters | Where it would come from |

### D.3 Methodological limitations

1. **Direct document access was unavailable** (§3.3). All verification ran through search against named primary documents rather than direct reading. The four Tier 1 documents in Appendix C.1 should be confirmed directly before external use.
2. **Currency and period mixing is a live hazard** in this dataset — FY2025 and H1 2026 figures are frequently reported adjacently, and savings targets appear in both GBP and USD. All figures here are labelled by period, and GBP is used throughout.
3. **Two quarters is a short series.** The recovery thesis (F1, F4, F5) rests on limited data. §11 states in advance what would overturn it.
4. **Industry billings estimates are not audited.** F5's values are directionally robust but should not be quoted as fact.
5. **No internal data was available.** Every question in §10.4 is answerable internally and none is answerable from outside.

## Appendix F — Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **RLPTC** | Revenue less pass-through costs. WPP's primary revenue measure, excluding costs incurred on clients' behalf and billed on without margin. The meaningful top line |
| **LFL** | Like-for-like. Growth excluding acquisitions, disposals and currency movement. The measure of underlying performance |
| **Headline operating margin** | Operating margin before restructuring, impairment and other non-headline items |
| **Elevate28** | WPP's turnaround strategy, announced 26 February 2026: single-company structure, four divisions, four regions, £500m gross annualised savings, growth from 2028 |
| **WPP Open** | WPP's AI-enabled marketing operating system, used internally and by clients |
| **WPP Open Pro** | Self-serve version launched 24 October 2025, for marketers working without a full-service agency. Access fee plus usage-based charging |
| **Enterprise Solutions** | WPP division for technology-led client services. Reported within WPP Creative until separate disclosure begins in 2027 |
| **Pass-through costs** | Third-party costs incurred on a client's behalf and rebilled without margin — principally media spend |
| **Organic growth** | Revenue growth excluding acquisitions and disposals. Used here for cross-group comparison |
| **COMvergence** | Independent agency tracking firm publishing new-business and billings rankings |
| **Outcome-based remuneration** | Commercial model paying for results achieved rather than time or resource consumed |
