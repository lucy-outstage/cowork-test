# The Open Door

### A go-to-market sprint to prove net-new demand for WPP Open

**Plansom · AI Impact Sprint — Engagement Design**
Version 1.0 · 6 October 2026
*Companion to: WPP Business Diagnosis · The Eighteen Months*

> **Name note.** "The Open Door" is a working name for the recipe — it plays on WPP Open and on opening a market WPP has never been able to reach. Rename freely; the method is what matters.

---

## The recipe in one page

Everything in the analysis reduces to three ingredients. Put them together and you have a business WPP does not currently have.

### 🥄 Ingredient one — A tail with real brand stakes that cannot buy WPP today

WPP's top 100 clients take roughly half its revenue. The ~1,600 disclosed clients below them average **£3.2m or less**, and beneath those sit thousands more too small to disclose. Outside WPP's book entirely is a far larger population: companies with brands they genuinely care about and no realistic route to agency-grade brand discipline.

They want the WPP factor. The fee has always made it impossible.

### 🥄 Ingredient two — Control as the wedge, not creativity

Open Pro's generation runs on Google's, OpenAI's and Adobe's models. So does everyone else's. **Creativity, speed and volume are commodity.** They are free, instant, and available to every marketer with a browser.

The one thing no general-purpose AI tool offers is **brand control** — one place where the brand's rules, assets, audiences, history and agents live together, so everything produced is on-brand, on-strategy and safe to publish.

WPP has thirty years of exactly that. Nobody else can claim it.

### 🥄 Ingredient three — A direct, radically simple motion

Outcome-based pricing is the long-term answer to the time-billing trap, and Cindy Rose has said it will *"take a few years."* **Net-new revenue is the fastest of the three escapes** and needs no pricing reform to work.

But tail economics only survive if the motion is simple: direct outreach, a fixed offer, self-serve onboarding, no pitch theatre. The UK Government paid £1.5bn for simplicity. The tail will not pay for complexity at all.

### The method

> **Take a defined set of net-new accounts. Reach them directly. Lead with control. Measure what converts.**
>
> Nothing about existing clients. Nothing about managed services. Nothing that needs a pricing committee.
>
> **One question: will companies that have never bought WPP buy WPP Open, and at what rate?**

## 1. Why this sprint, and why now

### 1.1 What the analysis established

| Finding | Implication for this sprint |
|---|---|
| Open works internally — 75,000 users, >90% of client-facing staff, revenue per head up 3.2% | The product is not the risk. **Demand is the risk** |
| Open Pro launched Oct 2025 for buyers who *"cannot afford a full-service integrated agency"* | The product for this segment already exists. No build required |
| Generation runs on rented, commodity models | The offer must be built on control, not capability |
| Production — the most platform-enabled division — is the only one growing | Platform-led delivery works where it has been applied |
| Outcome pricing *"will take a few years"*; JLR so far *"unique"* | Existing-client efficiency gains cannot be monetised yet. New logos can |
| UK Government bought **simplicity** for ~£1.5bn | Simplicity is a purchasable proposition, demonstrated at scale |
| Client retention 16% → 43%; Q1 −6.7% → Q2 −2.8% | The business has stabilised enough to attempt offence |

### 1.2 The single unanswered question

The diagnosis named six conditions the long-tail hypothesis depends on. **Test 1 is the critical path:**

> *Is Open Pro revenue incremental — reaching buyers who were never going to retain an agency — or substitutional, re-pricing clients WPP already has?*

Everything else is secondary, because if the answer is substitutional, the strategy inverts from growth into cannibalisation.

**You cannot answer that question using existing clients.** Any existing-client cohort is contaminated by the relationship. The only clean test is **net-new logos with no prior WPP revenue.**

That is what this sprint does, and it is the reason for every design decision that follows.

### 1.3 Why now specifically

- **Open Pro is a year old.** Early enough that the motion is not yet entrenched, late enough that the product is real.
- **The business has stabilised.** Offence is affordable in a way it was not twelve months ago.
- **Enterprise Solutions becomes separately disclosed in 2027.** When Open's commercial contribution becomes visible to the market, WPP needs an answer ready.
- **The Q3 update lands 29 October.** A sprint commissioned now reports into the planning cycle that follows.

## 2. The offer hypothesis

### 2.1 Value hypothesis

> **For** marketing leaders at brand-conscious companies between roughly £5m and £250m of revenue
> **who** care about their brand but cannot justify an agency retainer,
> **WPP Open Pro is** a brand control system
> **that** keeps everything they produce on-brand, on-strategy and safe to publish,
> **unlike** general-purpose AI tools, which give you infinite options and no judgement about which ones are right for *your* brand.
> **The proof is** that it is built and run by the people who hold brand discipline for the world's largest advertisers.

### 2.2 The three positioning variants to test

A sprint that tests one message learns one thing. Three variants, randomly assigned across the cohort, tell you which wedge actually opens the door.

| Variant | Lead message | Emotional driver | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Control** | *"Every asset on-brand. Automatically."* | Fear of brand damage | Control is the unmet need; buyers are anxious about AI output quality |
| **B — Standard** | *"Agency-standard brand work. Without the agency."* | Desire for quality | Buyers want the output standard, not the relationship |
| **C — Access** | *"The brand discipline behind the world's biggest advertisers. Now priced for you."* | Status and access | The WPP name is itself the draw — the "WPP factor" |

**What we learn:** which of the three actually produces replies and meetings. These are not cosmetic differences — they imply different products, different pricing and different competitive sets. Getting this wrong at scale is expensive; getting it right in six weeks is cheap.

### 2.3 Offer construct to test

The offer itself must be as simple as the message. Proposed shape:

| Element | Design | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry point** | Fixed-price starter tier, published, no negotiation | The tail will not enter a procurement process |
| **Onboarding** | Time-boxed brand setup — *"your brand live in 5 working days"* | Converts an abstract platform into a dated commitment |
| **First value** | A defined first deliverable inside the trial window | Gives the buyer something to show their boss |
| **Proof** | WPP credential stated plainly, UK Government win as evidence of scale | Borrows the brand without diluting it |
| **Exit** | Monthly, cancellable | Removes the risk that kills small deals |

> **Deliberate constraint.** No bespoke pricing, no scoping calls, no statements of work. The moment this offer needs a human to configure it, the tail economics fail. **If WPP cannot sell it without a negotiation, the hypothesis is already dead** — and finding that out in six weeks is a valuable result.

## 4. The must-test segment

### 4.1 Definition

**Net-new logo** means: no revenue relationship with any WPP company in the last 36 months, verified against WPP's own client master before contact. This verification is non-negotiable — it is the integrity of the whole test.

| Criterion | Specification | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | £5m–£250m | Below £5m: no marketing budget. Above £250m: likely already agency-served |
| **Brand stakes** | Consumer-facing or brand-dependent B2B | Control only matters to those with something to protect |
| **Marketing function** | At least one dedicated marketing hire | Someone must own the problem |
| **Current behaviour** | In-house + freelance + generic AI tools | The displacement target |
| **Geography** | **United Kingdom, phase one** | See 4.2 |
| **Exclusions** | Existing/former WPP clients; current agency-of-record relationships with a holdco; regulated sectors needing bespoke compliance | Keeps the test clean and fast |

### 4.2 Why the UK first

Three reasons, and the first is the strongest.

**It is WPP's weakest major market.** The UK declined **−7.6%** like-for-like in 2025 — worse than North America (−4.6%) or Western Continental Europe (−4.7%). The most to gain, and the least to cannibalise.

**The credential is local.** The UK Government chose WPP Media for a ~£1.5bn mandate on a simplicity argument. That is an unusually powerful proof point to put in front of a British mid-market marketer.

**Brand recognition is highest at home.** The "WPP factor" in Ingredient Two depends on the name meaning something. It means most here.

### 4.3 Target categories

| Category | Why they fit |
|---|---|
| Challenger CPG and food & drink | High brand stakes, high content volume, no agency budget. *Hawkstone is the proof case WPP already has* |
| Direct-to-consumer retail | Constant creative demand, performance-literate, used to self-serve tools |
| Mid-market hospitality and leisure | Brand-led, multi-location, chronically under-resourced |
| Regional divisions of global brands | Have brand guidelines to obey and no local agency budget — **control is their explicit problem** |
| Private-equity portfolio companies | Professionalising marketing fast, value simplicity, repeatable across the portfolio |
| Scaling technology companies | Brand-aware, tool-adopting, fast decisions |

### 4.4 Cohort design

**1,000 accounts, three cohorts.** Comparing motions teaches more than one large blast.

| Cohort | Accounts | Motion | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Cold direct** | 600 | Outbound to named contacts, no prior relationship | True cold conversion economics. **The primary test** |
| **B — Warm** | 200 | Partner, referral, network-sourced introductions | Whether warmth materially changes conversion |
| **C — Inbound** | 200 | Paid search and social against control-led messaging | Whether latent demand exists unprompted |

Within Cohort A, the three positioning variants split **200 / 200 / 200**.

Two to three named contacts per account — typically Marketing Director, Head of Brand, and the commercial owner.

## 6. What we measure

### 6.1 The funnel

Every account carries a cohort tag and a variant tag from first touch to invoice. Without that spine, nothing below is attributable.

```
  1,000 accounts  →  ~2,500 named contacts
         ↓
      CONTACTED          sequenced, 3-touch, multi-channel
         ↓
       REPLIED           any response
         ↓
   POSITIVE REPLY        expressed interest
         ↓
  QUALIFIED MEETING      ← PRIMARY METRIC
         ↓
     TRIAL START         brand set up in Open Pro
         ↓
      ACTIVATED          first asset produced
         ↓
        PAID             ← the number everyone wants, and the weakest read
```

### 6.2 Metric tiers

| Tier | Metric | Target | Reliability at this scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Qualified meetings per 100 accounts contacted | ≥3.0 | ✅ **Reliable** |
| **Primary** | Reply rate per contact | ≥5% | ✅ **Reliable** |
| **Secondary** | Variant performance (A vs B vs C) | Clear leader | ⚠️ **Large differences only** |
| **Secondary** | Trial starts | ≥25 total | ⚠️ **Usable, wide bands** |
| **Secondary** | Cost per qualified meeting | <£400 | ✅ **Reliable** |
| **Tertiary** | Trial → paid conversion | ≥25% | ❌ **Directional only** |
| **Tertiary** | Cost-to-serve per account | Baseline | ❌ **Directional only** |
| **Qualitative** | Stated deciding factor in every meeting | n/a | ✅ **High value** |

### 6.3 Statistical honesty

**What the sprint can conclude with confidence.** At ~2,500 contacts and an expected 5–6% reply rate, the reply rate is measurable to roughly ±1.2 percentage points. The qualified meeting rate across 1,000 accounts is measurable to roughly ±1.4 points. Both are solid enough to act on.

**What it cannot.** At these volumes the sprint will likely produce somewhere between **five and ten paid conversions in total**. That is far too few for a reliable conversion percentage. Anyone quoting "our trial-to-paid rate is X%" from this sprint is over-reading it.

**Variant comparison** needs a gap of roughly **4 percentage points** between variants to be statistically meaningful at 200 accounts each. The sprint will detect a clear winner; it will not resolve a close race. If A, B and C land within two points of each other, the honest conclusion is *"the wedge is not the deciding factor"* — which is itself a finding worth having.

> **The most valuable output at this scale is qualitative.** Twenty to thirty recorded conversations with net-new buyers, each asked directly what made them take the meeting and what would make them buy, will tell you more about whether control is the real wedge than any conversion percentage derived from eight data points. **Design for that, and treat the quantitative funnel as the frame around it.**

## 8. Systems

### 8.1 The measurement spine — the critical dependency

> **Every account carries `cohort_id` and `variant_id` from the source list through to the invoice.** If that identifier breaks at any handoff, the sprint produces activity data and no attribution. **This is the single most common reason go-to-market sprints fail, and it is an engineering problem, not a marketing one.**

```
 Source list → Sequencer → CRM → Product → Billing → Support
      └──────────── cohort_id + variant_id ────────────┘
                           ↓
                    Single dashboard
```

### 8.2 Systems required

| # | System | Purpose | Status to confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data & enrichment** (Apollo.io, ZoomInfo or equivalent) | Build the 1,000-account list with verified named contacts and firmographics | Plansom can operate directly |
| 2 | **WPP client master** | Verify net-new status before contact | ⚠️ **Access required from WPP — the hard prerequisite** |
| 3 | **Sequencing** (Outreach, Salesloft, Apollo) | Multi-touch delivery, variant assignment, reply capture | Seats needed |
| 4 | **CRM** (Salesforce or equivalent) | Account, contact, opportunity records with custom cohort fields | Config required |
| 5 | **Product analytics** (Open Pro) | Trial start, brand setup, activation, first asset, usage depth | Event access required |
| 6 | **Billing** | Paid conversion, revenue per account | Read access |
| 7 | **Support / ticketing** | Ticket volume and handling time — the cost-to-serve input | Read access |
| 8 | **Call recording** (Gong, Fireflies) | Every meeting recorded and transcribed for win/loss analysis | **Non-negotiable** — this is where the qualitative value lives |
| 9 | **Paid media** (Google, LinkedIn) | Cohort C inbound | Budget required |
| 10 | **Plansom** | Plan, tasks, prioritisation, standups, bottleneck reporting, progress | Plansom provides |
| 11 | **Dashboard** | One view of the funnel, daily refresh | Plansom builds |

### 8.3 Integration map

| From | To | Carries | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enrichment | CRM | Account, contacts, firmographics, `cohort_id`, `variant_id` | Week 2, one-time load |
| CRM | Sequencer | Contacts and variant assignment | Week 2, then continuous |
| Sequencer | CRM | Touches, replies, sentiment | Real-time |
| CRM | Product | Account identifier at trial start | On trial creation |
| Product | Dashboard | Activation and usage events | Daily |
| Billing | Dashboard | Conversion and revenue | Daily |
| Support | Dashboard | Tickets and handling time | Weekly |
| Call recording | Analysis | Transcripts tagged by account and variant | Per meeting |
| Everything | Plansom | Task state, blockers, progress | Continuous |

### 8.4 Integrity checks

Run weekly. Any failure stops the sprint rather than quietly corrupting it.

- **Net-new verification** — sample 10% of contacted accounts against the client master. **>5% false positives invalidates the test.**
- **Variant assignment** — confirm even distribution and no leakage between variants
- **Attribution continuity** — confirm `cohort_id` survives every handoff
- **Pricing discipline** — confirm no discounting or bespoke terms have been offered
- **Duplicate detection** — no account contacted in more than one cohort

## 10. Decision criteria

Agreed in writing at week zero, so the result cannot be argued with afterwards.

### ✅ Scale — proceed to a 10,000-account pilot

All of:
- Qualified meeting rate **≥3.0 per 100 accounts**
- **≥25 qualified meetings**, **≥10 trial starts**
- Cost per qualified meeting **<£400**
- A positioning variant clearly ahead, **or** a consistent qualitative signal on the wedge
- **<20% of meetings** requested bespoke pricing or scoping
- Net-new verification held at **>95% accuracy**

### 🔄 Pivot — the demand is real, the approach is not

Any of:
- Meeting rate ≥2.0 but offer construct consistently rejected
- One variant materially ahead of a weak overall result → re-test with the winner
- Strong Cohort B (warm) but weak Cohort A → the motion is wrong, not the market
- Buyers interested but the entry price is wrong in a consistent direction

### ⛔ Stop — the hypothesis does not hold in this form

Any of:
- Meeting rate **<1.5 per 100** across all variants after four weeks
- **>30% of meetings** require bespoke scoping → **the offer cannot be sold simply, and tail economics fail by definition**
- Cost per qualified meeting **>£1,000** with no path down
- **>20%** of supposedly net-new accounts had prior WPP relationships → data integrity failure, results void
- Buyers consistently say they would use a free tool instead → **control is not a wedge they will pay for**

> **A stop is a successful sprint.** It costs eight weeks and prevents WPP committing a year and a far larger budget to a motion that does not convert. Say so at the outset, so nobody is incentivised to manufacture a continue.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Client master access delayed or refused** | **High** | **Critical** | Gate 0 blocks the start. Escalate to sponsor on day one. Without it there is no incrementality test and the sprint should not run |
| Brand or legal approval slips | High | High | Front-load into week 0–1. Pre-agree credential wording before copy is written |
| Someone discounts to close a deal | Medium | High | No discount authority. Weekly pricing integrity check |
| SDR capacity gets pulled onto other work | Medium | High | Named, dedicated people. Sponsor protects the allocation |

## 13. What happens next

### If the sprint says scale

The 10,000-account pilot is a different exercise: it tests whether the motion holds at volume and whether cost-to-serve stays flat as accounts multiply. **That is where the tail economics are really decided**, and where simplicity stops being a positioning choice and becomes an operational requirement.

### If it says pivot

The most likely pivot, on the diagnosis, is from cold direct to **partner-led** — reaching the tail through the people who already serve them, rather than one at a time. Cohort B is in the design specifically so that signal is visible.

### The five tests this sprint does not answer

From the diagnosis, the long-tail hypothesis rests on six conditions. **This sprint attacks conditions 1 and 2** — incrementality, and whether control is a wedge buyers pay for. The rest remain open:

| # | Condition | When |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Cost-to-serve low enough for profitable scale | Partially at week 12; properly in the 10,000 pilot |
| 4 | Concentrating senior people on the top 100 improves those relationships | Separate workstream |
| 5 | The tail experience still feels like WPP | Needs volume and time |
| 6 | The segmentation line holds commercially | Policy decision, then observation |

## The recipe, restated

> **One.** There is a tail with real brand stakes that cannot buy WPP today.
> **Two.** Control is the wedge — not creativity, which is now free.
> **Three.** The motion has to be direct and radically simple, because outcome pricing is years away and tail economics die on complexity.
>
> **Mix:** take 1,000 net-new accounts, reach them directly, lead with control, measure what converts.
>
> **Serves:** one decision, in eight weeks, with the evidence to back it.
