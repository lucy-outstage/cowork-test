The Friday gate

Red has an action, not a conversation.

Agreed in writing at week zero, so a bad week produces a decision instead of a debate. Volume never fixes a wrong message.

Acceptance <35%

Targeting or profile problem. Re-check ICP fit and profile credibility before another invitation goes out.

Reply <8%

Message problem. Switch to the next positioning variant. Do not add volume.

Reply→meeting <20%

The offer is not landing, or qualification is too loose. Review the week's transcripts before changing anything else.

Connect <6%

Data problem. Re-source mobile numbers. Dialling more bad numbers changes nothing.

Meetings held <70% of booked

Confirmation is broken. Add same-day reminders before blaming the funnel.

≥2 LinkedIn restrictions

Pause the channel entirely. Restart at half volume per seat.

>30% asking for bespoke scoping

The offer cannot be sold simply. Tail economics fail by definition — this is a stop, and finding it in seven weeks is the win.

The operating rhythm

Fifteen minutes a day for most of the task force.

Every evening

Tomorrow's contacts, notes and call briefs are assigned to each person. Nobody plans their own day.

Every morning

A fifteen-minute standup, already assembled. Yesterday, today, blockers — no one types a status.

Every Friday

One hour against the gates, with the bottleneck report already written. Decisions, not updates.

The plan does not live in a document. It lives somewhere you can work.

Plansom is not only the team that unlocks the value here — it is where the how lives. Ten to twenty WPP people get invited into one controlled, simple environment that already holds the goal, the workstreams, the tasks, the owners, the agents and the gates. Nothing to adopt, nothing to configure, and nothing to roll out. On week seven WPP keeps the plan, the method and the evidence.
