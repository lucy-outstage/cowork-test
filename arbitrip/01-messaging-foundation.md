# Arbitrip — Messaging Foundation (core)

> The reference everything else is built on. If a post, carousel, or blog can't be traced
> back to a positioning, an audience, a pain, and a proof point on this page, it's not ready.

---

## 1. The big idea (category POV)

**Accommodation is the most underleveraged asset in travel.**

Every category of travel business treats accommodation as an afterthought — a line item, a
redemption nobody picks, an "add-on we'll get to later." Meanwhile it's the single biggest,
highest-frequency, highest-margin opportunity sitting in plain sight. Arbitrip exists to
turn accommodation from an afterthought into an advantage — for TMCs, loyalty programs, and
airlines alike.

This is the thread that runs through every post. We don't lead with "we have 1.5M hotels."
We lead with the problem: *you're leaving accommodation value on the table* — and then show
what changes when you don't.

## 4. Audiences

Three audiences, one common thread (accommodation), three *different* business reasons to
care. Tailor the value prop; keep the brand voice constant.

### A. TMCs & travel agencies — *bread and butter, largest sales focus*
- **Who:** Mid-market travel agencies and TMCs, leisure included (not restricted to business travel). 20+ travel agents as a rough signal.
- **Decision makers:** Owner (smaller TMCs), or head of commercial / head of product.
- **Sales context:** Inga's team in Europe; the deals already flowing. Largest pocket of targets.
- **What they care about:** Recapturing accommodation bookings they lose to OTAs; margin; transparency into what travelers actually book; not paying to build booking tech in-house.

### B. Loyalty programs — *the piece that connects all the dots*
- **Who:** New/challenger banks, credit-card issuers, digital wallets, fintechs, and retail clubs with (or wanting) a travel program. Rough signal: 50+ employees, 5,000+ members.
- **Decision makers:** Head of loyalty, head of marketing, commercial leads.
- **Sales context:** Jonathan (Barcelona) owns this; already bringing deals. Heritage proof: Bank Hapoalim "Poalim Wonder" (2021) → multi-million-member programs across Asia, Europe, North America.
- **What they care about:** Differentiating an undifferentiated program; lifting stalling activation/engagement rates; giving members something to redeem for *other than flights*.

### C. Airlines — *newer vertical, test & experiment*
- **Who:** Commercial carriers with loyalty programs. Sometimes folded under "loyalty," but a distinct vertical with its own team.
- **Decision makers:** Head of loyalty, head of ancillary/commercial.
- **Sales context:** Heating up — a new partnership with a leading Israeli travel/talent group is driving tens of bookings. Longer, more strategic sales cycle but lucrative. **Some specifics are still confidential — don't name partners publicly without sign-off.**
- **What they care about:** Ancillary revenue that isn't growing; accommodation as the obvious add-on that's "hard to execute"; capturing trip spend beyond the seat.

> **Why we don't worry about message dilution:** the pains are interconnected. Hotel people
> talk on airline threads; loyalty leads have opinions on TMC problems. Everyone wants to
> capture "the other thing." Accommodation leverage is the common ground that lets one brand
> voice speak to all three without sounding scattered.

## 7. Voice & tone

**North star:** *The Hustle* newsletter — conversational, fun to read, easy-going, accessible
English. Professional but human. Not a corporate deck, not travel-inspo.

**We are:**
- Plain-spoken and specific. Short sentences. One idea per line.
- Opinionated about the category (we have a POV on accommodation).
- Useful first — a reader should learn something even if they never buy.
- LinkedIn-native: strong hook, white space, a clear close.

**We are not:**
- Corporate / jargon-heavy ("synergistic end-to-end solutions" — no).
- Instagram travel-inspo ("how dreamy is Greece right now ☀️" — no). This is B2B.
- Over-formal or stiff, even on the company page.
- Hype without proof. Every bold claim earns its place with a stat or example.

**Calibration note:** earlier attempts to force this tone with raw AI output came out *too
extreme* (too jokey/casual). The target is the warm, smart middle — a sharp operator talking
to a peer, not a comedian and not a press release.

## 9. Post anatomy

Default structure for a single post:

> **Hook** → **Problem context** → **Insight (a human/industry POV)** → **Industry implication** → **Close (light CTA or question)**

- **Hook:** a sharp, specific line — ideally a pain or a counter-intuitive truth.
- **Problem context:** make the reader feel the cost of the status quo.
- **Insight:** the turn — what Arbitrip (or the founder) sees that others don't.
- **Implication:** why it matters for *their* vertical specifically.
- **Close:** a question, a soft CTA, or a one-line takeaway. Never a hard sell.

Carousels follow the same arc across slides: Slide 1 = hook, middle slides = context +
insight, last slide = implication + close/brand.

## 10. Guardrails (do / don't)

- ✅ **Do** tailor the value prop to the vertical's actual business pain (see §5).
- ✅ **Do** keep one consistent brand voice across all three audiences.
- ✅ **Do** repurpose: the monthly blog seeds one LinkedIn post; an Arbitrip page post can be reposted by Jonathan and vice-versa ("two hits" each).
- ✅ **Do** keep visuals on-brand and simple — quality over complexity.
- ⚠️ **Airline confidentiality:** don't name airline/agency partners or share specifics publicly without explicit sign-off.
- ⚠️ **Brand standard:** AI/Canva-level visuals are fine for Jonathan and simple posts; for higher-stakes Arbitrip page posts, confirm they clear the brand bar (test first, designer on standby).
- 🚫 **Don't** drift into Instagram travel-inspo or over-corporate stock-graphic territory.
- 🚫 **Don't** make a claim you can't back with §6 proof.
- 🚫 **Don't** let multi-vertical breadth turn into vague mush — anchor every post to one audience + one pain.
