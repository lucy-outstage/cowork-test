## How to read this document

This is a **validation pass** over the original diagnosis, not a rewrite of it. Three things changed:

1. **Every number was re-checked.** Verdicts are in §1. Most of the original held up. Some was mislabelled. One conclusion was materially incomplete.
2. **The timeframe moved.** The original draft mixed full-year 2025 with half-year 2026 figures without labelling which was which. WPP has reported twice since the data in the original draft was assembled.
3. **The story changed direction.** The original reads WPP as a business in decline. On the evidence below, WPP stopped declining somewhere around March 2026. That is a different diagnosis and it points at a different sprint.

> **A note on sourcing.** This container could not open PDFs or investor-relations pages directly — outbound fetches to `wpp.com`, `sec.gov` and most publisher domains were blocked by the network proxy. Everything here was verified through search against named primary documents (WPP's RNS announcements, the 2025 Annual Report on Form 20-F, the H1 2026 results statement) and corroborating trade press. **Where a figure rests on a single secondary report, it is marked.** Confidence ratings are in §9. If you need audit-grade figures, pull the three primary PDFs listed at the end.

### ⚠️ The coincidence trap

The original draft reports **−5.4%** twice, for two unrelated things:

- FY2025 revenue less pass-through costs declined **−5.4%** like-for-like
- WPP Media's **H1 2026** revenue declined **−5.4%** like-for-like

Same number, different year, different scope. Anyone reading quickly will merge them and conclude the whole business is still falling at 5.4%. **It is not.** See §2.

### What the original draft missed entirely

Six material facts, each of which changes the diagnosis:

- **Quarterly trajectory.** Q1 2026 −6.7% → Q2 2026 −2.8% LFL. The decline roughly halved in one quarter.
- **Client retention.** 16% → 43% year on year.
- **Account flow flipped.** 2025 losses of ~$6.98bn → 2026 losses of ~$2.81bn, against >$3bn won.
- **The UK Government account.** ~£1.5bn over four years — WPP's largest single win, taken from Omnicom.
- **WPP re-entered the FTSE 100** on 1 October 2026, ten months after being demoted.
- **Net debt of £2,935m** against a market capitalisation of roughly £4.1bn.

## 2. The number that reframes everything

The original diagnosis rests on half-year averages. Half-year averages hide the shape of the year.

| Period | RLPTC, like-for-like |
|---|---|
| FY 2025 | **−5.4%** |
| Q1 2026 | **−6.7%** |
| Q2 2026 | **−2.8%** |
| H1 2026 (blended) | −4.7% |

**Read the quarters, not the half.** WPP's revenue decline nearly halved between January and June 2026. The H1 figure of −4.7% is an average of a bad quarter and a much better one, and it describes a business that no longer exists.

Three further readings matter:

**Margin stopped bleeding while revenue was still falling.** H1 2026 headline operating margin was 8.4%, **up 0.2pp** like-for-like, on revenue down 4.7%. Holding margin through a revenue decline is harder than growing it in an upturn — it means cost reduction is running ahead of revenue loss, not chasing it. FY2025 margin fell 15.0% → 13.0%. FY2026 guidance is 12–13%. The two-point annual slide has become roughly flat.

**Productivity per person is rising.** This is not disclosed; it is calculable, and it is the most useful number in the dataset:

| | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue less pass-through costs | ~£5,026m | £4,745m | −5.6% |
| Average headcount | ~106,000 | ~97,000 | −8.4% |
| **RLPTC per head (half-year)** | **~£47,400** | **~£48,900** | **+3.2%** |

*Calculated indicator. H1 2025 RLPTC is derived from the reported −5.6% decline; headcount is WPP's average, not period-end; portfolio disposals affect both lines. Directional, not audited.*

WPP is producing **more revenue per person than a year ago**, while shrinking. That is the closest thing in the public record to evidence that the AI investment and the restructuring are doing what they were meant to do. It is also the number WPP does not publish — which is itself worth asking about.

**Where the market landed.** Shares fell ~62% across 2025 on two profit warnings; market capitalisation went from roughly £24bn in 2017 to ~£3.1bn at the 2025 trough. WPP left the FTSE 100 in December 2025 after almost thirty years. It returned on **1 October 2026**, market cap ~£4.1bn, after a share-price recovery of more than 30%.

Be precise about what that means. WPP re-entered partly because index places opened up through takeovers of other companies (Beazley, Schroders), not purely on its own merit. **It is a recovery, not a vindication** — the company is still worth about a sixth of its 2017 peak.

## 3. Client economics, corrected

The original draft divides total revenue by 1,700 disclosed clients and gets ~£6m. It then correctly warns the figure is not a true average. The warning is right but understates the problem — the £6m number points in the wrong direction and should be dropped.

**What the disclosures actually support:**

| Grouping | Share of RLPTC | Implied revenue per client |
|---|---|---|
| Top 10 clients | 21% (~£2,137m) | **~£214m** |
| Top 100 clients | ~50% (~£5,088m) | **~£51m** |
| **Clients 101–1,700** | **≤50% (~£5,088m), shared with all sub-£500k clients** | **≤£3.2m — and realistically well below** |

**The meaning:** WPP's client base is not a gentle slope, it is a cliff. A top-ten client is worth roughly **70 times** a client in the long tail. The ~1,600 clients outside the top 100 average at most £3.2m each, and less once the thousands of sub-£500k clients drawing on the same pool are counted.

Two consequences:

- **Concentration is the dominant commercial risk.** Losing one top-ten relationship costs more than losing several hundred tail clients. The 2025 Coca-Cola and Mars media losses are the demonstration.
- **The tail is Open Pro's natural market, and it is already inside the building.** WPP does not need to find the segment Open Pro is built for. It already serves roughly 1,600 of them at an average of ≤£3.2m, on contracts cancellable at 90 days. That is both the opportunity and the hazard in §5.

## 4. What the leadership actually committed to

**Elevate28** (26 February 2026) is more specific than the original draft conveys. Rose's own diagnosis: *"our recent underperformance has been driven by excessive organisational complexity, a lack of an integrated operating model and inconsistent strategic execution."*

| Commitment | Detail |
|---|---|
| Structure | Holding company → **single company**. Four divisions: WPP Media, WPP Creative, WPP Production, WPP Enterprise Solutions |
| Geography | Four regions: North America, Latin America, EMEA, APAC |
| Cost | **£500m annualised savings** |
| Phasing | Stabilise new business and cut cost → integrate go-to-market, return to organic growth **during 2027** → simpler, lower-cost, AI-enabled growth **from 2028** |

Two points the sprint should hold onto.

**The client need and the internal programme are the same need.** When WPP Media won the UK Government account (~£1.5bn over four years, taken from Omnicom), the buyer's stated rationale was consolidating suppliers from **33 to 23**, cutting planning times and management costs. The customer bought simplification. That is external validation that "streamline and simplify" is not just an internal efficiency story — it is a proposition clients are actively purchasing.

**Enterprise Solutions — the likely growth engine — is currently unmeasurable.** It is reported inside WPP Creative. Separate like-for-like disclosure begins **in 2027**. Anything the sprint concludes about Enterprise Solutions before then rests on management commentary, not reported numbers.

**Leadership continuity is a live risk.** Rose arrived September 2025. Matt McNeany joined from Omnicom in July 2025 to run WPP Open. Jon Cook — VML global CEO and CEO of WPP Creative, thirty years in the business — leaves in March 2027, inside the window in which Elevate28 must prove itself. Eric Campbell succeeds him. Cook's exit lands in the same quarter Elevate28 promises a return to organic growth.

## 5. WPP Open: the honest picture

### What is established

| Evidence | Status |
|---|---|
| 75,000+ internal users; >90% of client-facing staff | Company-reported. **Internal adoption, not customers** |
| Nestlé and Coca-Cola using it | Company-reported |
| 14 hrs/week saved for a team of four (≈90 days/yr capacity) | WPP's own pilot sample |
| Strategy & creative: four weeks → three hours (unnamed tech brand) | WPP marketing claim |
| Hawkstone Harvest IPA: 33× asset volume, four-person team, two days | WPP case study |
| ~£300m AI/data/Open investment (2025); $400m five-year Google commitment | Confirmed |
| Open Pro launched 24 October 2025; access fee plus usage-based charging | Confirmed |

### What is not established

No separate Open revenue. No ARR. No paid-client count. No contribution margin. No public price list. No lifetime build cost. **This is not evidence of weakness — it is simply unmeasured from outside, and it will stay unmeasured until the 2027 disclosures.**

### Three findings the original draft did not reach

**① Open adoption does not protect the relationship.**
Coca-Cola is a named WPP Open client. Coca-Cola also moved its North American media business to Publicis. Platform adoption and assignment retention are separate outcomes, and WPP has a worked example proving it. Any sprint hypothesis that treats Open as a retention mechanism needs to explain this case first.

**② Open Pro is built on the technology of its own competitors.**
Open Pro's generation runs on Google Imagen and Veo, OpenAI DALL-E and Sora, Adobe Firefly; text on Gemini, Claude and ChatGPT. WPP has committed $400m to Google over five years. Trade coverage already notes Open Pro competing against Google's and Canva's own marketing tools.

So: **WPP pays its competitors for the inputs to a product that competes with them.** The models are commodity and cannot be the moat. Whatever defends Open has to be the layer WPP alone owns — proprietary audience and brand data, brand governance and safety, and the pull-through into managed services. This is the direct answer to "without watering down the brand": *Open Pro's defensible value is brand control, not content generation.* Generation is available everywhere, often cheaper.

**③ Open Pro's addressable market overlaps WPP's own tail.**
Open Pro is positioned for marketers who want to work *"without the help of an agency"* and for clients who *"cannot afford a full-service integrated agency."* WPP's own tail — ~1,600 clients averaging ≤£3.2m, on 90-day terms — fits that description closely.

That creates a genuine strategic question the sprint must answer early: **is Open Pro reaching new money, or re-pricing existing money?** Management states it expects Open Pro revenue to be incremental, with managed-service pull-through also incremental. That is the stated intention. It is not yet evidence. On 90-day contracts, a tail client offered a self-serve tool at a fraction of a retainer has an obvious and immediate option.

## 6. The three needs, diagnosed

### Need 1 — Satisfy customers: WPP Open and better work/production delivery

**Diagnosis: the evidence is already in, and it is being under-exploited.**

WPP Production grew **+1.6%** LFL in H1 2026 (Q2: +1.3%) — the **only** growing division while Media fell 5.4% and Creative 4.9%. Production is also where Open's benefits are most measurable: the Hawkstone case, the Google model access claiming up to 70% faster production and doubled asset utilisation.

But Production is **5% of revenue less pass-through costs.**

> **WPP's fastest-growing capability is its smallest, and it is the one where its AI investment demonstrably works.**

Meanwhile the customer-satisfaction metric that matters most has already moved: **retention 16% → 43%**, with Huawei, Tesco and L'Oréal renewed. That is the clearest signal that delivery is improving — and it is a leading indicator of revenue, not a lagging one, which is precisely why guidance points at growth in 2027 rather than 2026.

### Need 2 — Streamline and simplify

**Diagnosis: correctly identified by WPP's own leadership, externally validated by buyers, and at risk of consuming the capacity needed to prove it.**

Rose named complexity as the root cause. Elevate28 targets £500m of savings and a single-company structure. The UK Government bought simplification explicitly. The thesis is sound and the market agrees.

The risk is execution load. WPP is simultaneously: restructuring four divisions into four regions, removing £500m of cost, absorbing ~9,000 fewer people year on year, replacing the CEO of its largest creative network, and commercialising a new platform — while Publicis compounds **+5.5%** organic growth and the merged Omnicom–IPG (closed 26 November 2025) integrates.

Net debt of **£2,935m** against a **~£4.1bn** market cap means roughly 70p of debt for every £1 of equity value. There is limited room to spend the company's way out of a sequencing problem.

### Need 3 — Maximise WPP Open without watering down the brand

**Diagnosis: the tension is real, it is structural, and it resolves through segmentation, not volume.**

Open Pro's own positioning — campaigns *without an agency*, for clients who *cannot afford* one — is a disintermediation product sold by an intermediary. Pointed at the wrong segment it cannibalises; pointed at the right one it extends reach.

The resolution follows from §5②: **WPP's brand is not its ability to make content.** Generation is commoditised and WPP rents it from Google, OpenAI and Adobe. WPP's brand is its ability to make content that is *strategically right and safe to publish at scale* — audience intelligence, brand governance, and accountability for outcomes.

That gives a clean dividing line to test:

- **Open Pro should sell** *brand-safe execution capacity* to buyers who were never going to retain an agency.
- **WPP managed services should sell** *judgement, accountability and integration* to buyers who need it.

The JLR mandate is the signal worth watching: a global integrated media **and** creative partnership with **outcome-based remuneration**. When WPP is paid for outcomes, Open stops being a cost-saving device that deflates fees and becomes margin WPP keeps. **Outcome pricing is the mechanism that makes efficiency accretive rather than dilutive.** Without it, every hour Open saves on a time-based contract is revenue WPP bills away.

## 8. Revised sprint hypothesis

**The original hypothesis:** *WPP Open has demonstrated adoption and delivery benefits; the commercial opportunity is to turn those benefits into a simple offer a defined segment understands, buys and uses profitably.*

That is sound and should survive. But it assumes WPP's core problem is still revenue decline. On the Q1→Q2 evidence, the decline is already being addressed. The sharper framing:

> **WPP has proven Open works internally — 75,000 users, rising revenue per head, margin held through a revenue decline. It has not proven Open creates value it can keep. The risk is not that Open fails; it is that Open succeeds at making WPP more efficient on contracts that pay for time, converting productivity gains into lower fees rather than higher margin.**
>
> **The sprint's question is therefore commercial-model design, not product adoption.**

### What to test

| Question | Why it matters | Where the answer is |
|---|---|---|
| Which customers pay for Open today, and under what arrangement? | Establishes the real revenue model | Internal billing — available now |
| Is Open Pro revenue incremental or substitutional? | Decides whether growth is real | Client-level revenue history, pre/post adoption |
| Does Open-enabled delivery raise or lower effective fee rates? | Tests whether efficiency is accretive or dilutive | Compare Open-delivered vs. traditional accounts on margin per hour |
| Which use case most consistently produces a valued result? | Identifies a credible offer | Pilot data behind the 14-hour and 33× claims |
| Where do qualified buyers stop progressing? | Locates the acquisition obstacle | Open Pro funnel since 24 Oct 2025 |
| What do onboarding and support cost per account? | Tests whether tail growth is profitable | Cost-to-serve on Open Pro cohort |
| Can outcome-based pricing extend beyond JLR? | The margin-retention mechanism | JLR contract structure as template |

**First decision for the sprint:** which offer to examine — Open Pro for independent client use, or Open-enabled managed service. Different buyers, different economics, different journeys. **The §5③ overlap suggests examining them together, because the risk lives in the gap between them.**
