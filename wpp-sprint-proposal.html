01 Where WPP is

It already turned. Most people missed it.

Half-year reporting averages a bad quarter with a much better one. The quarters tell a different story: between January and June 2026 the rate of decline more than halved.

Revenue less pass-through costs, like-for-like. WPP preliminary results, February 2026, and interim results, 6 August 2026.

16% to 43% year on year, with Huawei, Tesco and L'Oréal renewed.

02

Up 0.2 points like-for-like while revenue was still falling. Cost reduction running ahead of revenue loss.

03

People got more productive.

Revenue per employee up roughly 3.2%. WPP does not publish this. It can be derived.

02 The trap underneath Open

Every benefit WPP publishes for Open is time saved.

Fourteen hours a week returned to a team of four. Four weeks of strategy compressed into three hours. Thirty-three times the asset volume. Up to 70% faster production. And WPP bills by time.

01

The better it gets, the smaller the invoice.

Under a model that bills for time, every hour Open saves is revenue WPP no longer earns. The efficiency goes to the client. WPP's reward for building the platform is a smaller fee.

02

Rose has already named it.

Her stated reasoning is that time and materials is not sustainable long term, because AI lets the work be done faster with fewer people. The diagnosis is right.

03

The fix is years away.

Rose has said outcome-based pay will "take a few years", and has described JLR as so far unique in accepting it. In the meantime, a mixed economy of business models.

Creativity is free.
Control is not.

Open Pro's generation runs on Google's, OpenAI's and Adobe's models. So does everyone else's. The one thing no general-purpose tool offers is a single place where a brand's rules, data, history and agents live together — so everything produced is safe to publish.

03 The opportunity

The fastest new revenue is from people who were never going to hire you.

Repricing existing clients waits on outcome-based contracting. Reaching buyers who never had an agency does not. It is the only route to new revenue that needs no pricing reform at all.

01

A cliff, not a slope.

WPP's top ten clients take 21% of revenue. The top hundred take roughly half. A top-ten client is worth about 67 times one in the tail.

02

The tail is already inside.

Around 1,600 disclosed clients outside the top hundred average £3.2m or less, on contracts commonly cancellable at 90 days.

03

The product already exists.

Open Pro launched in October 2025, built for buyers who cannot afford a full-service integrated agency. Nothing needs to be built.

One goal

Acquire paying WPP Open Pro customers who have never bought anything from WPP.

A single outcome, measurable inside seven weeks, and the one place where AI has the most commercial leverage in WPP's business right now.

One obstacle

WPP has no motion for reaching buyers who were never going to retain an agency — and no evidence the demand exists.

Rose named complexity as the root cause. This is its commercial consequence: a company organised entirely around large relationships, holding a product built for small ones, with no way to connect the two.

One measure

Qualified meetings per 100 net-new accounts contacted, and trials started, by the end of week seven.

Signed off by the sponsor against a baseline of zero, because this motion does not currently exist. Net-new means no WPP revenue relationship in 36 months, verified before contact.
